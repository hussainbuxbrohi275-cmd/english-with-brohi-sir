Use "has" with he, she, it, and singular nouns: "She has a book." "The dog has a bone."

Use "have" with I, you, we, they, and plural nouns: "I have a book." "The dogs have bones."

Quick trick: if you can replace the subject with "he/she/it," use has. If you can replace it with "they/we," use have.

Present Simple is for habits and facts: "I walk to school every day."

Present Continuous is for actions happening right now: "I am walking to school right now."

Common mistake: saying "I am agree" instead of "I agree" — feeling verbs like agree, know, and like are almost never used in continuous form.

1. "He don't like it" → should be "He doesn't like it"

2. "I am understanding it" → should be "I understand it"

3. "More better" → should be simply "better"

4. "Discuss about" → should be "discuss" (no "about" needed)

5. "Since 3 years" → should be "for 3 years"
